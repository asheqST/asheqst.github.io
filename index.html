03 / Research

How systems earn trust, and how people defend them.

My research interests lie at the intersection of cybersecurity, artificial intelligence, and human interaction with complex technological systems. I am particularly interested in how systems establish trust, identify abnormal behavior, remain accountable, and support effective human decision-making.

Research directions

  1. 01

    Trustworthy and secure AI systems

    How AI agents establish identity and accountability, and how assistants behave across whole conversations.

  2. 02

    Critical infrastructure cybersecurity

    Detecting attacks on cyber-physical systems by reading network and physical signals together.

  3. 03

    Human-Cybersecurity Interaction

    How people perceive, decide, and act on security, and how systems and training can support them.

  4. 04

    Cybersecurity education and workforce development

    Hands-on, evidence-based training that builds the capability of the people who defend public infrastructure.

04 / Teaching

Learning by doing, reasoning from evidence.

I teach security the way it is practiced: inside realistic environments, working from evidence rather than assumptions. Learners should be able to break things safely, explain what they found, and leave with skills they can use the next working day.

  • Scenario-based and hands-on

    Learners practice inside realistic, isolated environments where it is safe to break things.

  • Evidence-based reasoning

    Conclusions come from what the logs, packets, and endpoints actually show.

  • Responsible AI

    Using AI tools safely is taught as part of everyday security practice.

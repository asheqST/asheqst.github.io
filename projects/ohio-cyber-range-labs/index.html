Ohio Cyber Range Labs

Hands-on cybersecurity education for public-sector resilience.

Overview

Reusable cyber-range environments and hands-on labs that bring Ohio local-government IT teams to security-operations readiness.

Problem and motivation

Counties, cities, school districts, and utilities face the same threats as large enterprises, often with a small IT staff and no dedicated security team. Ohio expects these organizations to run cybersecurity programs, and training through the state’s Ohio Persistent Cyber Improvement (O-PCI) program is a recognized way to meet that expectation.

Approach

Learners work inside realistic, segmented networks that mirror the environments they manage, with live detection and investigation tools. The labs follow a connected storyline, from segmentation and intrusion detection through log analysis, investigation, containment, and response, and are mapped to MITRE ATT&CK and NIST. The O-PCI Gateway 2 IT Track turns these labs into a structured pathway for local-government IT professionals.

Architecture

  • Virtualized environments. Each lab runs on VMware vSphere with segmented networks behind pfSense and VyOS, so learners see the same boundaries they defend at work.
  • Network monitoring. Security Onion, Suricata, Zeek, and Arkime give learners alerts, protocol logs, and full packet capture to investigate.
  • Log analysis. Telemetry is centralized in OpenSearch and Kibana, so an investigation can move from one alert to the full picture.
  • Endpoint visibility. Wazuh and Velociraptor show what happened on the hosts themselves and support evidence collection.
  • Infrastructure as code. Terraform and Ansible provision every environment, and snapshots return it to a clean state between cohorts.

Technical design considerations

  • Realism over slides. Exercises reflect how local-government IT departments actually operate.
  • Repeatable by design. Every cohort gets an identical, clean environment.
  • Two audiences. Hands-on labs for technical staff, and tabletop exercises for leadership.

Learning design

  • Safe to break. Learners attack and defend inside isolated environments that reset between sessions.
  • Evidence over intuition. Exercises ask learners to show what the logs, packets, and endpoints prove before they act.
  • Mapped to real frameworks. Every exercise ties back to MITRE ATT&CK and NIST, so skills transfer to day-to-day work.
  • Responsible AI. Labs address AI security and the responsible use of AI tools as local governments adopt them.

Outlook

Public-sector resilience depends on the people who run small IT shops. Training that is practical, repeatable, and recognized by state law scales that capability across an entire state.

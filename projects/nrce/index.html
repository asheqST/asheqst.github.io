NRCE

Abstract

A camera system that captures scenes as editable 3D data instead of flat video, so objects, lighting, and timing can be changed after the shot.

Keywords

Computer visionNeural renderingSensor fusionPython

The problem

A conventional camera records a flat image, and once the shot is taken its contents are fixed. Film production, robotics, and digital-twin work increasingly need scenes they can measure, edit, and re-render.

Approach

NRCE proposes fusing three sensors into a single synchronized stream: a high-resolution color camera, a LiDAR depth sensor, and an event camera. The stream is encoded into a neural, volumetric representation of the scene that can be re-rendered, with objects moved, lighting changed, and timing adjusted after capture. AI models separate the scene into objects and fill gaps in the depth data. The software pipeline is developed against simulated sensors before any hardware commitment.

Design principles

  • Data, not pixels. The output is a computable scene rather than a video file.
  • Simulation first. The pipeline can be built and tested before hardware exists.
  • Composable outputs. Segmented objects can be reused across scenes and tools.

Privacy and integrity

  • Captured spaces are sensitive. Volumetric scans can include people and private places, so captured data is encrypted and access-controlled.
  • Consent and provenance. Editable reality raises authenticity questions, so provenance of the original capture should be preserved.
  • Misuse. Realistic re-rendering can be abused, which argues for clear labeling of edited output.

Outlook

Capturing the world as data rather than images opens new workflows in film, robotics, and digital twins, and it raises new questions about privacy and authenticity that are best addressed in the design, not after release.

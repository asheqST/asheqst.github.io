Smart-grid cyber testbed

Abstract

A co-simulation testbed that attacks a simulated power distribution grid and detects those attacks from network and grid telemetry together.

Keywords

Critical infrastructureGridLAB-DModbus TCPMachine learning

The problem

The power grid increasingly runs on networked control systems built for reliability, not security. An attacker who can inject false measurements or disrupt control traffic can cause physical consequences.

Approach

The testbed couples a GridLAB-D model of a distribution grid with a simulated industrial control network. Denial-of-service, false-data-injection, and man-in-the-middle attacks are run against it, and machine-learning models learn to detect them by correlating network behavior with electrical measurements across more than ten million records. The work was part of DOE Grid Modernization Laboratory Consortium research conducted in collaboration with Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL), and it formed the basis of an MS thesis.

Design principles

  • Physics plus packets. Detection uses network and power-system signals together.
  • Realistic attacks. Scenarios target the industrial protocols the grid actually uses.
  • Reproducible experiments. Co-simulation makes every attack repeatable.

Security considerations

  • Stealthy attacks. False-data injection can look normal on the network, so physical measurements are needed to expose it.
  • Defense in depth. Zero Trust, role-based access, segmentation, and SIEM approaches were evaluated for cyber-physical systems.
  • Safe testing. Simulation allows destructive scenarios that could never be run on a live grid.

Outlook

As grids add distributed energy and smart meters, the attack surface grows. Detection that understands both the network and the physics is essential to keeping power systems resilient.
